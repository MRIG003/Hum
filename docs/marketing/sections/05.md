## 5. Activation

### Current state

Engage has two people to activate, and they have very different experiences:

- **The host** has to create a meeting, share a link, get people to join, and launch something worth answering. That's a lot to ask in the first five minutes of a meeting they're also running.
- **The attendee** has it easy: no login, a clear disclosure, one tap to react. This side is already excellent.

What hurts activation today:
1. ~~Cold start.~~ Fixed: the Render Starter plan keeps the server always on.
2. **A first-time host still has to decide what to do.** *Ask the room* now offers ready-made questions, which solves most of the "empty console" problem. What's missing is a sense of *when*: which prompt to use at minute 5, 30 and 60 of a training session.
3. **No definition of "activated"**, and no counters to measure it.

### Activation defined

> **An activated host** has run at least one meeting where **3 or more attendees joined** and **at least one prompt** (poll, pulse, recall check or word cloud) got answers, and the host either opened the summary or received it by email.

Measured as: activated hosts ÷ hosts who created a meeting, counting **only hosts whose 14-day window has closed**. Hosts still inside their 14 days are *pending*, not failed. Report them separately.

**Attendee activation:** share of joined attendees who take at least one action. The PRD target is above 70%.

### The plan

**Move 1 — Keep the first load instant (done; keep checking).**
`render.yaml` now uses the always-on Starter plan. After the redesign deploys, check a cold load of the homepage, `/try` and the Meet panel from a new browser profile in under 3 seconds. The new homepage has a 3D hero, so watch its weight on slow connections. *Tool:* Render MCP to confirm the plan and watch response times.

**Move 2 — Session plans for the beachhead (weeks 3–6, lighter than v1).**
Ready-made questions cover the *what*. Session plans cover the *when*. Build three, all for trainers and facilitators; team-lead plans come later. These can start as a public page or PDF (a lead magnet, §4) before they become a product feature.
- **Training session (60–90 min):** opening pulse check, recall check after each agenda block, a word cloud for "one thing you'll use", closing pulse.
- **Workshop (90–180 min):** icebreaker word cloud, prompts per agenda topic, a Focus moment reminder at the start of exercises, Q&A open throughout.
- **Onboarding / enablement:** recall checks tuned for "did the point land", with "Missed that" answers flagged in the coach summary.

One tap from the create form ("Start from a template") gets the host a full agenda with prompts already saved. *Skills:* `onboarding`, `copywriting`.

**Move 3 — The first-meeting path.**
After "Create meeting", show one short checklist with three items, nothing more: *Paste the link in your meeting chat → Wait for 3 people to join → Launch your first pulse check.* Use the existing in-page patterns, with no pop-ups (the principles test forbids them in meetings anyway). The checklist goes away after the first meeting. *Skill:* `onboarding`.

**Move 4 — Concierge first meetings for design partners.**
For the first 10 hosts, offer a 10-minute setup call and join their first session as a silent attendee if they agree. This is product research disguised as onboarding. It also catches the problems no test finds (a corporate firewall blocking WebSockets, a Meet admin setting, a facilitator who shares the wrong screen). Write up what you see after each session. *Skill:* `customer-research`.

**Move 5 — Founder welcome email and two onboarding emails (account holders only).**
Resend is already wired. Three plain-text emails from the founder's name:
1. **On account creation:** "Thanks for trying Engage. Reply and tell me what meeting you're running it in. I read every reply."
2. **Before the second meeting (2 days after the first, if no second meeting):** one tip from the template for their meeting type.
3. **After the first meeting with ≥3 attendees:** the summary (already sent) plus one question: "Did the engagement chart match what you felt in the room?"

*Indicator:* reply rate (target above 20% at this stage). *Skill:* `emails`.

**Move 6 — Attendee join conversion.**
The PRD's join-rate target is above 60% of invited. Small moves: make the chat message the host pastes shorter and clearer ("Open this in a second window for polls and questions: [link]"), test it in real meetings, and check that the join page loads fast on corporate networks. *Skills:* `copywriting`, `cro`.

**Move 7 — Marketplace install → first meeting.**
For Marketplace installs, the first screen in the Meet side panel should offer "Try it with your next meeting" and a 30-second explanation of what attendees will see. Measure installs → first meeting with ≥3 attendees. *Skill:* `onboarding`.

| Weeks | Ship |
|---|---|

| 3–4 | Three trainer templates, first-meeting checklist, welcome email |
| 5–8 | Onboarding emails 2 and 3, chat-message copy test, concierge sessions for hosts 4 to 10 |
| 9–12 | Meet-panel first-run screen, first activation-rate read on closed 14-day windows |

### 12-month activation outlook

- **Q1:** Activation measured for the first time. Target: 50% or more of hosts who create a meeting reach activation (small numbers, so read it as direction, not truth).
- **Q2:** Templates for team leads (standup, weekly review, all-hands) added as the second audience starts arriving.
- **Q3:** In-product prompts suggest the next template based on meeting length and agenda.
- **Q4:** Calendar-created meetings (Phase 2 integration), so the host doesn't have to remember to start Engage at all.
