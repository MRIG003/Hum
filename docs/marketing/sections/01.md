## 1. Executive summary

**What this plan optimizes for:** proof that trainers and facilitators come back to Engage week after week, with one founder, about $10 a month, and no paid ads.

Engage is built. It has a complete, tested product, a Google Meet™ add-on, a live demo, and a Marketplace listing awaiting review. What it doesn't have is a single outside host. So the first 90 days aren't about traffic. They're about ten real hosts running real sessions and coming back. Every channel after that depends on what those ten teach us.

### Three big bets

**1. Trainers and facilitators first, not "everyone who runs meetings."**
Of the four audiences Engage could serve, trainers and facilitators have the biggest problem and feel it most often. The session *is* their product, they run several a week, and many already pay $10–25 a month for Mentimeter or Slido themselves. Engage's sharpest features were made for them: the live engagement chart, dip suggestions, the recall check with "Missed that", and the coach summary. Team leads come second (Q3), educators later, webinar hosts not this year.

**2. Distribution through the Google Workspace Marketplace and through the meetings themselves.**
The Marketplace is where Meet users already look for add-ons, and approval is close. Every Engage meeting also shows the product to 5 to 50 attendees, some of whom run meetings of their own. A single calm line on the attendee recap ("Run your own meeting with Engage, free for Google Meet™") turns each session into a demo. With no ad budget, these two channels can grow without growing the founder's hours. Content and directories support them. Hand-recruited pilots start them.

**3. Free in the meeting, paid for what happens after.**
Everything inside a meeting stays free, which is the promise on the listing and the engine of the loop. Pro, priced just under Mentimeter at a hypothesized $9–12 per host per month, adds meeting history and trends, unlimited summaries and exports, templates, larger meetings and shareable results. Pro launches only when the evidence says so: 25 weekly active hosts, 40% six-week retention, and five hosts who said they'd pay.

### What twelve months looks like, plausibly

- An owned domain, always-on hosting and an approved Marketplace listing with 10+ honest reviews
- About 120 weekly active hosts, mostly trainers and facilitators, with team leads arriving through attendee spillover
- The attendee recap loop and Marketplace search as the two biggest sources of new hosts
- Pro live since Q3, with $500–1,500 MRR by month 12. This is a hypothesis with wide error bars, not a forecast.
- A measured cost per host, in hours and dollars, for every channel
- A clear answer on whether to stay bootstrapped or raise, based on growth and retention rather than hope

### 90-day priorities

1. **Remove the blockers (week 1–2):** always-on hosting so the first load is instant, an owned domain and email, the Marketplace review chased, and the positioning question decided (named "who's gone quiet" vs. a room-first message).
2. **Run 3 pilot meetings by week 4 and reach 10 active hosts by week 12,** through 25+ personal invitations and concierge first sessions.
3. **Measure what matters:** privacy-safe aggregate counters, the feedback survey, and a north star of *Weekly Engaged Meetings*.
4. **Make the first meeting easy:** three trainer templates, a first-meeting checklist, and a founder welcome email.
5. **Start the compounding assets:** the attendee recap line, founder-led LinkedIn, 6 honest comparison and how-to pages, and directory listings.
6. **Launch once, when it's earned:** Product Hunt and Show HN in week 11 or later, only with Marketplace approval, always-on hosting and three hosts willing to be quoted.
