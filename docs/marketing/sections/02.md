## 2. Strategic frame

### What Engage is, in one sentence

Engage shows a meeting host, while the meeting is still running, whether the room is with them, and gives them one quick thing to do about it when it isn't.

That sentence is assembled from the homepage ("See who has gone quiet in your meeting, while it is still running") and the product plan ("people contribute throughout instead of for about 5 minutes"). The second half, the suggestion when engagement dips, is the part no polling tool offers, and it should stay in every version of the line.

### The category we're claiming

Engage sits inside an existing, crowded category: **audience engagement tools** (Mentimeter, Slido, Poll Everywhere, AhaSlides, Wooclap, Kahoot, plus native polls in Meet, Zoom and Teams). Buyers already know the category, and that helps. They search for "polls for Google Meet" and "Mentimeter alternative", and they already pay $10 to $25 a month per presenter for it.

Engage should not try to invent a new category. It should **redefine one corner of the existing one**:

> Polling tools help you *ask* the room something. Engage tells you *how the room is doing* while you still have time to fix it.

The proof points that make this credible are all built already: the live engagement chart, dip detection with a named suggestion, the recall check with a "Missed that" answer, and the coach summary. Polls, Q&A and word clouds are table stakes. They should be listed, but they aren't the reason to switch.

### The market-quality gate: problem size × frequency

| Audience | Size of the problem when it happens | Frequency | Quadrant |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trainers and facilitators (corporate L&D, freelance workshop leaders, onboarding trainers) | **Large.** The session *is* their product. A flat room means a bad review, a lost client, or training that didn't stick | **High.** Several sessions a week, often 1 to 3 hours long | **Best quadrant** |
| Team leads and managers (recurring standups, reviews, all-hands) | Small to medium. A dull meeting is annoying, rarely costly | Very high. Daily | Habit-forming, but easy to drop on price and easy to forget |
| Educators (higher-ed lecturers, adult education, online tutors) | Medium to large | High during term | Strong, but with blockers: admin-controlled installs, student-privacy rules, budget cycles |
| Webinar and event hosts | Large per event | Low to medium. Monthly or quarterly | Expensive to stay top-of-mind. Larger audiences (200+) are untested |

**Verdict:** the core problem sits in the best quadrant **for trainers and facilitators**, and only there. For team leads it is a small, frequent problem, which can still produce habits and retention but makes paying for it a harder sell. This is why the beachhead below is not "everyone who runs meetings."

### Who we're for (ICP, distilled)

The founder named four audiences. A solo founder with no budget can win one first. The plan sequences them:

1. **Beachhead (Q1 to Q2): trainers and facilitators who run long sessions on Google Meet.** This means in-house L&D trainers, onboarding and enablement leads, and freelance facilitators running workshops of 8 to 50 people, 60 to 180 minutes long.
   - *Stated problem:* "I can't tell if people are with me when cameras are off."
   - *Real problem:* "I find out the session didn't land after it's over: in the feedback form, or when nobody remembers it a week later."
   - *What they're buying:* confidence mid-session, plus a summary they can show a client or manager.
   - *Why first:* biggest pain, highest frequency, already pays for Mentimeter-style tools out of their own pocket, and every session exposes Engage to 8 to 50 new people, some of whom run meetings themselves.
2. **Second (Q3 onward): team leads at companies on Google Workspace** who run recurring meetings. They come through the Marketplace and through attendees who saw Engage in a training. They are the retention engine (weekly meetings), not the revenue engine.
3. **Later (Q4+): higher-ed and adult-education instructors.** These are reachable through the same Meet add-on. K-12 is out of scope for 12 months because of minors' data and school admin controls.
4. **Not in this plan: webinar and event hosts.** Larger audiences, external attendees, and webinar platforms with built-in engagement. Revisit after load testing to 200+ and after paid plans exist.

### The business model logic

Today Engage is free and listed as free on the Marketplace. The logic the plan works toward:

- **Free for the core in-meeting experience**, forever. The free product is the acquisition channel: every meeting is a live demo to every attendee.
- **Paid per host (Pro)** for the features that matter after the meeting and across meetings, such as coach summaries over time, recurring-meeting trends, templates, CSV and summary history, larger meetings, and branding removal. Competitor anchors are Mentimeter at $11.99 to $24.99 per presenter per month, Slido at $17.50 per month, and Poll Everywhere from $10 per month.
- **Team or Org later** (SSO, admin, retention controls). This is the Phase 3 "organization layer" in the product plan.

The compounding thesis: **attendee exposure is the channel.** A facilitator running four sessions a week of 20 people shows Engage to about 80 people a week. If even a small share of those people run meetings themselves, the product markets itself in a way content alone never will. Everything in §4 and §7 is designed to make that loop visible and measurable.

### Brand voice (the non-negotiable)

Engage already has a clear voice in its README, product rules and homepage. Codified:

**Tone:** calm, plain, honest. Short sentences. It reads like a thoughtful colleague, not a growth-hacker.

**YES:**
- Plain verbs: *see, ask, check, pause, start*
- Exact claims about what is measured: "Engage only sees what people do inside Engage"
- "The meeting always wins"
- Naming limits openly ("not tested in a real Chrome window yet")

**NO:**
- Surveillance language: *track employees, monitor attention, productivity score, catch people not paying attention*
- Hype: *revolutionize, supercharge, AI-powered*, exclamation marks
- Gamified bragging: leaderboards, "top participant"
- Fear-of-missing-out CTAs or countdowns

**CTA rules:** "Start a meeting", "Try the live demo", "See what attendees see". Never pressure.

**Trademark rule:** "Google Meet™" with the footnote wherever Google's product name appears in Marketplace-facing copy. Google rejected the first listing over this.

### The positioning question, and how the new homepage answers it

The product's founding rules say *"Show aggregate trends, not people"* and *"nobody feels monitored"*. The product shows the host **a named list of who has gone quiet**, and the summary email lists names with no activity.

The October redesign handles this well. The headline is now **"Every voice in the meeting, not just the loudest few."** That frames the named list as a way to *include* people, not to watch them. The homepage FAQ says plainly that only the host sees it and that participants are told before they join, and game scores are private with no ranking. This is close to the middle path v1 recommended: lead with the room and inclusion, and keep per-person detail as a disclosed host feature.

What's left to keep consistent:
- The Marketplace short description leads with tools ("Live polls, questions, reactions and quick games"), not the inclusion promise. That's fine for search, but the first line of the detailed description should carry "every voice".
- In any outreach, video or post, the quiet-person moment must show the host **inviting people in**, never calling someone out. The demo video kit enforces this.
- Revisit after pilots. If attendees in the survey say Engage felt like monitoring, the named list becomes opt-in per meeting.

### The name

"Engage" is a common word, and Slido's paid tier is also called Engage. Nobody will find the product by searching its name for a long time. The plan doesn't recommend a rename. The cost is high, the new logo has just been made, and there's no evidence yet that it's needed. It recommends a **fixed descriptor everywhere: "Engage for Google Meet™"** (and "Engage, every voice in the meeting" off-Meet). Engage now has a real address, `engage.neuronlab.in`. Because it sits under `neuronlab.in`, links and authority go to the NeuronLab domain. That's fine as long as Engage stays there.
